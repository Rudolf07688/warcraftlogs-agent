Went well

  • Decide, then post the reason. Every tier deviation, deferral and design choice is on the board with its reason. I could review all of them after the fact without asking anyone.
  • Honest limits. Ansel flagged that the realm rule can't stop short phrases before QA found it. Tomas logged the per-turn wait budget as backlog rather than stretching scope.
  • Peer coordination without me. Tomas warned Ansel about the conftest fixture, and Ansel warned everyone about the env leaking into shells after the reboot (#31). Both were right, and neither needed arbitration.
  • Lighter tiers held. There was no SM and no Spec Kit for 037, and footprints were never breached.

Went badly, and the lesson

  • Unreviewed judgment calls in the security fixes. Both High QA findings came from 037 decisions that looked sound in isolation: srcset was never considered, and the full policy for may_switch=False was unintended. My merge spot check read the diffs but didn't try a bypass. Lesson: for a security fix, my merge check includes one bypass attempt, and the QA sweep is the real gate. Keep it at the end.
  • Tier S over-applied. I chose S for security-labelled tickets to save ceremony. That was right on cost, but it put six security changes into one stream with no second look before merge. Lesson: tier S for security is fine only with the sweep as the gate, so say that explicitly in the brief.
  • Hand-off routing. After the crash the developers' completion notices went to team-lead, not to me. Team-lead had to relay them three times.
Net: autonomy cut owner-facing traffic to zero and kept the sprint moving through a crash. What it costs is fewer second looks before merge. The end-of-sprint QA sweep paid that back, at the price of one rework round.
