How the autonomy grant went

  • Decide, post the reason, move on worked. Zero owner questions and zero escalations. Every judgment call (tier choices, realm rule, rightmost-XFF, bulk writes, process-wide 429 memory) is reviewable on the board after the fact.
  • Agents scaled the ceremony down themselves. Maren picked tier M and S below the default table, said why, and named the compensation (a Postgres test, the QA sweep as the gate).
  • The price was fewer second looks. Both QA Highs (srcset bypass of sc-157, web search waiting through the full retry schedule) were in tier-S security code nobody reviewed before the sweep. The sweep caught them and one round fixed them, so the system worked, but the lesson in Maren's and Tomas's memories is "self-review against neighbouring invariants" and "one bypass attempt in the merge spot check", not more asking.
  • Agents flagged their own weak calls. Ansel named two decisions he'd reconsider (realm shape rule, bulk writes) and filed the stronger fix as backlog; Ansel also reported his own possible live call after the reboot.

What went wrong, and what I did

  • Owner UI command interrupted the PO mid-launch at 19:54; it relaunched both developers, leaving two dead duplicate transcripts. Cost: small. My bad: I'd suggested /agents, which no longer exists.
  • The machine crashed at ~20:13Z. Git, the board and every uncommitted file survived. One developer transcript ended in 4 KB of NUL bytes; I backed it up, stripped the line, and resumed all three agents with their context. About 15 minutes lost.
  • After the resume, completion notices routed to me instead of Maren. I forwarded each hand-off in one message. Worth knowing for the next crash.
  • A VS Code setting injected .env into every shell, including secrets and unexpanded ${…} placeholders. Ansel spotted it and moved the whole team to clean-env test runs; the owner turned the setting off.
